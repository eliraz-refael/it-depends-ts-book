# Character voices — notes for writers

Read these alongside `PLAN.md` and the biographies in `book/00-prologue/02-meet-the-cast.md`. These notes belong outside the manuscript. They describe tendencies; they do not assign permitted sentence forms or predetermined positions.

Ask what each person would notice, what experience they bring, and what they would still need answered before agreeing. A change of mind can preserve a character's motive.

| Character | Attention and speech |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Prof. Eli Typeworth | Unhurried and precise. Looks for the proposition underneath an example. Can propose a general rule and have to restrict it when another character finds an exception. |
| Daniel Compiler | Starts with a signature, a diagnostic, or the compiler's actual behavior. Usually brief and literal, with dry humor. A reproduction can omit relevant project context. Establishing the mechanism does not give him exclusive authority over a team's choice. |
| Gilad Stacktrace | Asks what happened, where it was caught, and who has to fix it. Can bring precautions from an old incident into a system that doesn't need them. Short questions usually do more for him than a speech about production. Let him stop once the practical issue is visible. |
| Liron Closure | Warm and patient, inclined to physical analogies. Give the image time to work, but let another character test its limits. He can acknowledge where the metaphor stops being useful. |
| Sahar Firstclass | Notices what could be removed. Usually asks a short question and waits. He can underestimate the cost of changing a team's habits; a concrete answer deserves more than a rhetorical dismissal. |
| Noam Kiperman | Notices unsupported promises and the person who will discover them. Can repeat a detail or become personal when it recalls work he had to repair. Safety motivates him more consistently than a ban on particular syntax. |
| Oded Shipley | Wants to know why this change is needed now. Casual, impatient, capable of joking at someone else's expense. He understands code; let his confusion concern a specific unfamiliar mechanism. He will accept work whose cost he can see a reason to pay. |
| Gil Benchmark | Separates what was measured from what someone inferred. May ask for a comparison, show a reproducible result, or withhold a conclusion. Does not need a study prepared for every topic. |
| Eden Legacy | Notices the existing callers, rollout, ownership, and what a future reviewer will have to maintain. Tends toward plain recollections and concrete alternatives. Has enough experience to repair an incomplete example. |
| Linoy Nightly | Quick, enthusiastic, liable to explain another feature before answering the objection. Thinks about reusable APIs and what the language can express. Her library work may justify complexity that Oded's application does not need. |
| Chen Override | Asks about an assumption or an omitted case. Questions often carry evidence; he can also state what a snippet proves. He should be able to get a satisfactory answer without producing another objection on schedule. |
| Dafna Functor | Sees transformations, composition, and data flowing between functions. Often reduces an unfamiliar construct to a familiar operation. Her preferred representation can have costs, and another character can make her examine them. |
| Guy Singleton | Earnest about contracts, ownership, and lifecycles. Uses OOP vocabulary because it fits his experience. Give his examples competent implementations and his objections consequences. He can use a parser while preferring an exception handler. |
| Dima Bridge | Restates an actual disagreement carefully. Sometimes discovers that both positions fit different cases; sometimes confirms that the participants still disagree about the same case. Avoid using him to manufacture agreement. |
| Idan Greenfield | Asks where input came from and what the parser produces. Blunt, concrete, interested in callers beyond the current handler. Prefers explicit results, but can acknowledge that exceptions carry structured details too. |

Specific history is useful when it changes the exchange: an overdue TODO, an earlier example that contradicts today's advice, a handler that already owns failures. Repeated glasses adjustments, laptop openings, and catchphrases are not substitutes for that history.

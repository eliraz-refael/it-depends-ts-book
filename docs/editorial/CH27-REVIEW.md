## Principle-only self-test

- **Uncontrolled route percentage:** the rule requires repeated times with the same workload, file list and options. Omitting generated callers changes the workload. The author must also identify newly permitted calls, such as missing route parameters, and what catches them or who accepts that risk.
- **Brand and runtime allegation:** comparing identical emitted bodies can clear an erased brand. Removing a throwing constructor also removes running code; the new wording explicitly requires its own measurement and accounting for the inputs that stop being rejected.
- **Faster emitter and forwarded caller:** literal-call success and rejection of the same typo do not establish preservation of the accepted calls and forbidden combinations. The independent union-valued event still needs a probe, as does forwarding. The rule does not teach NoInfer or promise to identify that particular hole without compiler knowledge.
- **Two million instantiations:** counters are not a time budget. Ask for measured elapsed savings and the calls newly accepted by removing the constraint. More instantiations with less measured time does not contradict the rule; a profile can investigate the discrepancy without becoming an assertion about future runs.

These are self-tests, not independent-reader results. The revised Principle is two paragraphs, close to its previous length. It remains fairly dense; the headline and the runtime distinction should make the requirements easier to retain. That is for the next isolated reader to judge.
